Reading *The Stranger* is uncomfortable because Meursault does not offer the emotional signals that would make him easy to place.

The people around him—and eventually the machinery judging him—seem almost more disturbed by his failure to perform grief correctly than by the event he is accused of.

## The performance of meaning

We often say we want honesty, but social life depends on familiar gestures. There are expected ways to mourn, love, apologize, and explain yourself. Refusing those gestures can look inhuman, even when performing them would be dishonest.

That does not make Meursault admirable. It makes the space around him revealing.

The book left me thinking about how quickly we confuse visible emotion with moral truth, and how desperately we want every life to form a story that makes sense.

I am not sure the universe owes us that story. I am also not sure we can live without trying to create one.
