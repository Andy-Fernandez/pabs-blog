*Aftersun* feels less like watching a story and more like trying to remember one.

Nothing announces itself as the important moment. A song, a camera pointed in the wrong direction, the light from a hotel room, a father dancing badly—small images gather weight only after they are gone.

## Understanding too late

Sophie is looking back with information she did not have as a child. That is what made the film hurt for me. Love does not automatically give us the ability to understand the people we love.

Sometimes we receive the meaning of a moment years after the moment itself has disappeared.

The film never turns Calum into a puzzle that can be solved cleanly. It lets him remain a person: warm, playful, frightened, exhausted, and partly unknowable.

> Memory is not a recording. It is an edit we keep making because we want one more chance to look.

I finished the film thinking about all the ordinary moments whose importance I may only understand later.
