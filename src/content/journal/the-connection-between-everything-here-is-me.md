I don't want to build a traditional portfolio where only finished projects, technologies, and things designed to prove competence are allowed to exist.

I don't want a blog trapped inside one subject either.

I want a small corner of the internet where I can leave traces of my intellectual and creative life: things I build, things I learn, films that obsess me, books that make me think, food I prepare, experiments that go wrong, questions I still cannot answer, and thoughts I may no longer share three years from now.

> The site should not show a finished identity. It should show an identity in motion.

One day I may be obsessed with software. The next day I may write about a film, share something I cooked, or record an idea that arrived while I was walking through La Paz.

They do not need to belong to the same category.

**The connection between everything here is me.**

## Why it should feel handmade

The fanzine, photocopy, cutout, handwritten-note, tape, mistake, and overlap language makes sense to me because it lets the site feel made by a person—not produced by a marketing department.

I imagine a table holding an open book, a movie ticket, a handwritten recipe, a drawing, a piece of code, a photograph, a three-in-the-morning idea, and a project that does not yet know what it will become.

Everything coexisting.

## A form of memory

Five or ten years from now, I want to open `/2026`, read what I thought about a film, visit one of my first projects, see what I was learning, and meet an earlier version of myself.

That turns the page into something more interesting than a portfolio.

It becomes memory.

This is not only a record of what I made. It is a record of my process of becoming someone.
