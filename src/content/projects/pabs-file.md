## The idea

I wanted a personal website, but not the kind that freezes a person into a headline, a stack of technologies, and a neat row of finished work.

The problem was not how to make another portfolio. The problem was how to make a place spacious enough for software, films, books, cooking, unfinished thoughts, and whatever I care about next.

## The decision

The site has only two real content systems: **Projects** for things I intentionally build, and **Journal** for everything I want to document or reflect on.

The visual language borrows from fanzines, photocopies, marked-up pages, and objects left together on a desk. The roughness is not decoration pasted over a portfolio template. It is part of the argument: a personal website can feel personal.

## What I am learning

The hardest part is knowing when expression becomes noise. The homepage can overlap, tilt, shout, and surprise. A long journal entry needs to settle down and let the words breathe.

This project is active, which is another way of saying that it is allowed to change as I do.
